Satisfactory Tips and Tricks – 50 Things I Wish I Knew Sooner

Early Game Tips (Tier 0-2)

Once you unlock a Constructor, stop hand-crafting anything you’ll ever need again. Even a small run of 20 iron rods is better produced automatically because you’ll need thousands later; a single Constructor removes repetitive clicks and frees you for layout planning and exploration. Prioritize early milestones that give you vertical movement and clearing tools, because the time saved on travel and vegetation directly multiplies your productive time.

Treat your first base as a learning tool, not a permanent solution. Most players demolish their starter hub once they understand how belts, conveyors, and foundations fit together; that demolition is expected and useful because it reveals spacing mistakes and throughput errors. Keep a stock of foundations, walls, and a few basic machines in your inventory so you can prototype improvements where you find resource nodes or want to expand.

Use biomass only as a bridge to coal and fuel. Biomass is cheap to collect but inefficient long term, so don’t build sprawling biomass farms. Unlock the chainsaw early to clear trees and rocks faster, and rush the jetpack at Tier 5 because vertical mobility changes how you design stacks, reach nodes, and move between production floors.

Building and Layout Tips

Always build on foundations to create a consistent grid; machines placed on the ground will misalign belts and make future expansions messy. Foundations give you exact spacing, making Conveyor Lifts and stair placements predictable and ensuring parallel belt runs keep the one-foundation clearance you need for maintenance. That single foundation of space between parallel lines saves hours when you need to swap out a machine or re-route a belt.

Stack production vertically instead of sprawling outward when possible. Vertical factories compress travel time and centralize power, piping, and conveyors; Conveyor Lifts snap cleanly to holes in foundations so you can run belts between stacked floors without clipping issues. Place walkways and glass walls so you can monitor throughput and access machines quickly—glass shows blockages at a glance and catwalks let you service machines without walking on belts.

Color-code your areas to reduce cognitive load during crises: pick a color for iron processing, another for copper products, and a third for steel lines. The visual separation makes it faster to identify the right belt or splitter under stress, and hypertubes are worth building to connect distant sections since they cut travel time dramatically and keep you in the production flow.

Belt and Logistics Tips

Belts are the factory’s lifeblood, and mismatching tiers creates hidden bottlenecks: Mk.1 belts carry 60 items per minute while Mk.4 carries 480, so feeding Mk.4 machines from Mk.2 lines will choke throughput. Use splitters and mergers liberally to balance inputs across identical machines; they cost nothing in build resources and prevent one machine from starving while another overflows. Programmable Splitters let you filter items on mixed belts, which is essential once lines carry screws, plates, and rods together.

Design overflow paths to prevent cascading stoppages. Route surplus into storage containers or the AWESOME Sink so upstream machines keep working instead of jamming belts and stopping production. When you hit mid-game, use belt balancer blueprints for equalized distribution across multiple lanes, because manual balancing fails as production scales.

Mark belt speeds and important lines with signs so you don’t confuse Mk.2 and Mk.4 in the heat of a rebuild. Conveyor Lifts and proper vertical routing make multi-floor logistics manageable, but maintain labeled routes so future-you or teammates can trace supply at a glance.

Power Tips

Always do the power math before you build more machines: sum the wattage of machines and compare it to your generators so you avoid surprise blackouts. Segment your grid with Power Switches so a problematic array can be isolated without collapsing production across the map. Geothermal generators on geysers provide free baseline power and should be built as soon as you find a geyser, because they require no fuel beyond the initial construction.

Coal and fuel require supporting infrastructure you must plan for: each coal generator needs a connected water source, and fuel generators need refinery chains that convert crude to fuel. Don’t build large coal or fuel arrays without pump placement and refinery capacity mapped out, because water access and refinery bottlenecks are the real limits, not coal or crude availability. Power Storage units buffer spikes and smooth demand by discharging when consumption exceeds steady generation, which reduces the need for oversized generator arrays when you have short production peaks.

Nuclear is powerful but complex; treat it as a high-output step after you understand fuel-based systems and waste handling. Until then, focus on building stable fuel production and scattered geothermal sites to cover base load, and only expand into nuclear once you have the layout discipline to manage reactors and heat safely.

Production Efficiency Tips

Machines essentially run full speed or sit idle, so feeding them the correct inputs prevents wasted cycles and power. If a Constructor sits at partial input, you lose efficiency; either redesign the feeder so it runs at 100% or remove the machine from the line. Before adding extra machines, identify whether your bottleneck is raw node extraction, belt throughput, or power, because throwing machines at the problem can compound the issue.

Underclocking is a practical power-management tool: running a machine at 50% clock often reduces power draw by more than half while still producing at a stable rate, so underclocking over-supplied machines can be cheaper than adding new generators. Overclocking offers higher output but consumes power exponentially, so only overclock when your grid can support the draw or when a few overclocked machines are cheaper than doubling production lines.

Calculate exact machine ratios from the wiki before building chained production, because many recipes require precise proportions to run continuously. Alternative recipes unlocked from Hard Drives can change ratio math dramatically—screw and plate alternatives are the common examples—so explore crash sites early to see which recipes alter your layout needs. The AWESOME Sink is your overflow outlet: feed excess items into it for tickets and coupons instead of letting belts jam.

Exploration Tips

Scan with the Object Scanner on every trip because it reveals resource veins, crash sites, and Hard Drives that unlock alternate recipes and blueprints. Mark discovered nodes and sites on the map so you don’t re-explore areas by accident; a marked pure iron vein or coal seam prevents wasted trips and helps you plan rail or hypertube routes. Keep a chainsaw and jetpack for efficient clearing and vertical access so you can evaluate nodes quickly without long detours.

Crash sites frequently contain Hard Drives that unlock recipe alternatives, and dedicated exploration runs to snag those drives often pay off more than minor factory tweaks. Hostile creatures respawn, so clear routes and return with caution—don’t assume an area is permanently safe. Vehicles and drones extend your reach: place fuel stations along vehicle routes to avoid long walks, and consider drones late-game for long-distance, belt-free transport once you can afford them.

The map contains multiple biomes with different resource mixes, so explore beyond the starter area to find lead, oil, and geothermal geysers that your planned expansions will need. Planning extraction hubs near key nodes saves long conveyor runs and keeps train routes shorter when you adopt rail logistics.

Multiplayer Tips

Dedicated servers handle uptime and large factories far better than client-hosted sessions, so use a dedicated host for serious multiplayer builds. Assign roles early—power, logistics, and research—and keep responsibility boundaries to avoid duplicating work or starving critical systems. Clear rules for infrastructure changes prevent accidental breakages; always announce demolitions or major reworks because one person’s “cleanup” can knock a teammate’s production offline.

Communicate small changes in chat or voice to avoid misunderstanding; a quick message before rerouting power or belts saves hours of troubleshooting. Agree on shared resources like geothermal sites and train stations to stop conflicts over node claims, and use map markers liberally so everyone knows primary routes and critical nodes. When scaling, coordinate test windows for major overclocks or train additions so you can observe effects without disrupting daily operations.

If you want reliability under load, centralize large, high-throughput systems on dedicated servers and reserve client sessions for exploration or small builds. The reduced latency and crash resistance make large-scale rail, nuclear, and drone networks less risky.

Advanced Tips

Manifolds and load balancers each have roles: manifolds are simple and store items to feed phased consumption, but they take time to fill and recover from demand spikes. Load balancers equalize flow instantly across lanes; they’re compact and ideal for high-throughput parallel machines but require precise layout. Use manifolds where buffering is acceptable and load balancers where immediate, equal distribution is necessary.

Trains scale throughput infinitely compared to belts once you accept the signaling complexity. Learn block-based signaling and station scheduling to avoid collisions and to keep trains on time. Blueprint Mode is your friend for large changes: place ghost buildings to test spatial fit and collision with existing belts before committing resources, and keep a manual save before flipping a major switch or rolling out a new layout.

Test changes in small stages rather than swapping an entire production line at once, because the cost of an error grows with scale. Use manual saves and staged rollouts to revert quickly if unexpected bottlenecks appear. Remember that each production upgrade enables the next, so plan growth hierarchically and validate each layer before scaling.
